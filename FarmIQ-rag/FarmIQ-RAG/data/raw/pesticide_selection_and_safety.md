# Choosing and Applying Crop Protection Products

FarmIQ Starter Reference — how to select and apply a crop protection product
("medicine") safely and legally.

This document explains **how to choose** a treatment. It deliberately does not
name brands, active ingredients, or doses: the legally permitted crops, pests,
dose, waiting period, and safety instructions for any product are printed on
that product's label and in your State Agricultural University's Package of
Practices (PoP). Confirm there — or with your KVK or block agriculture officer —
before you spend money on a spray.

## Step 1 — identify the problem before you treat

A spray only works if it targets the actual cause. The same symptom can come
from an insect, a fungus, a bacterium, a virus, a nutrient shortage, or water
stress.

- Identify the pest or disease first: look at the underside of leaves, growing
  tips, flowers, and the base of the stem; inspect early in the morning when
  insects are sluggish.
- Check whether the cause is **biotic** (pest, disease) or **abiotic**
  (nutrient deficiency, drought, waterlogging, heat, spray drift, salinity).
  Abiotic problems do not respond to pesticides at all.
- Viruses (for example yellow mosaic, leaf curl) cannot be cured once a plant
  is infected. The response is to control the insect vector and remove
  infected plants.
- If you cannot identify the cause, ask your KVK before buying anything.

## Step 2 — use the least-risk option first

Work up this order and stop as soon as the problem is controlled:

1. **Cultural** — remove and destroy infected plants and crop residue, destroy
   weed hosts, adjust spacing, avoid excess nitrogen, rotate crops.
2. **Mechanical** — hand-pick egg masses and larvae, use traps and barriers.
3. **Biological and botanical** — conserve natural enemies, use biopesticides.
4. **Chemical** — only when the above are not enough, or the pest population
   has crossed the economic threshold.

## Step 3 — match the product to the pest, not to the shop's advice

- **Insecticides** control insects; **fungicides** control fungal disease;
  **bactericides** control bacterial disease; **herbicides** control weeds;
  **nematicides** control nematodes. They are not interchangeable. A fungicide
  sprayed for an insect problem wastes money and adds residue.
- Different products affect the insect in different ways. **Contact** products
  must land on the insect or the surface it walks on. **Systemic** products are
  taken up by the plant and reach insects feeding inside it — those are the
  ones that work on sap-sucking pests hidden in growing tips.
- Match the stage. Products that work on young larvae may fail on
  fully-grown caterpillars or on eggs, and eggs are often unaffected by
  insecticides.
- Sucking pests (aphids, whitefly, jassids, thrips, mites) and chewing pests
  (borers, caterpillars, cutworms) need different choices.

## Reading the label — the only authoritative dose

- **Label claim:** the label lists exactly which crops and which pests the
  product may be used on. Using it on an unlisted crop is both unsafe and
  illegal.
- **Dose and spray volume:** follow the label figure for the crop and pest.
  "More medicine" does not mean better control — over-dosing damages the crop,
  increases residue in the produce, kills beneficial insects, and speeds up
  resistance.
- **Pre-harvest interval (PHI):** the minimum number of days between the last
  spray and harvest. Selling before this period can leave illegal residue and
  get the produce rejected at the market.
- **Re-entry period:** how long to stay out of the field after spraying.
- Check the registration or licence number and the manufacturing and expiry
  dates. Buy only from a licensed dealer and keep the bill.

## Mode of action and resistance

Repeated use of one product lets the surviving pests breed, and the population
becomes resistant — the spray stops working.

- Note the **mode of action** group printed on the label (insecticides are
  grouped by IRAC, fungicides by FRAC, herbicides by HRAC). Rotate between
  *different* groups instead of repeating the same one.
- Do not use the same product every time just because it worked once.
- Do not mix two products unless the label permits that mixture. Unapproved
  mixing can cause crop injury and loss of effect.
- Broad-spectrum products kill natural enemies, which is often followed by a
  worse outbreak of the same pest or a new one (resurgence and secondary pest
  outbreak).

## Timing and application

- Spray in the cooler part of the day — early morning or late evening — and
  when wind is low. Midday heat causes evaporation and drift, and the spray
  can burn the crop.
- Do not spray immediately before expected rain; if it rains too soon, the
  product washes off and you must judge whether the crop is still protected.
- Do not spray when the crop is in full flower if a spraying will reduce
  pollination — protect bees. Never spray on flowering weeds or near beehives.
- Calibrate the sprayer before use: check the nozzle output, replace worn
  nozzles, and use the correct water volume. Poor coverage on the underside of
  leaves is one of the most common causes of "the medicine did not work".
- Keep the spray pattern overlapping and keep the recommended distance from
  the nozzle to the plant.
- Herbicides need extra care: pre-emergence products need soil moisture and a
  weed-free, clod-free seedbed; post-emergence products need the correct weed
  stage. Herbicide drift damages neighbouring crops, and sprayer tanks must be
  cleaned very thoroughly (or kept separate) before reuse.

## Lower-risk and biological options

- **Neem-based** products and botanical extracts.
- **Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt)** — useful against caterpillar larvae that eat
  treated leaf surface.
- **Trichoderma** — soil and seed treatment against soil-borne fungal disease.
- **Fungal biocontrol agents** such as *Beauveria bassiana* and
  *Metarhizium anisopliae* — contact action against some insects.
- **Pheromone traps** for monitoring and mass trapping (fruit borer, fall
  armyworm), and yellow or blue sticky traps for sucking pests.
- Biopesticides are lower-risk but not risk-free and not instant. They work
  best as part of a planned programme, applied before the pest builds up,
  with correct timing and coverage.

## Protecting yourself, other people, and the environment

- Wear the protective equipment the label requires: coveralls, gloves, boots,
  goggles or face shield, and a mask or respirator. Do not spray in bare feet,
  shorts, or with bare hands.
- Never eat, drink, smoke, or rub your eyes while handling or spraying. Wash
  hands, face, and clothes with soap after work, and wash spraying clothes
  separately from family clothes.
- Keep children, pregnant women, and animals away from the field during and
  after spraying. Do not let anyone enter before the re-entry period.
- Do not spray against the wind, and never spray near a drinking water source,
  well, pond, canal, or livestock shed.
- Store products in their original labelled container, locked away, away from
  food, fodder, seed, and water, and out of children's reach.
- Never transfer a product into a food or drink bottle.
- Empty containers must not be reused for food or water. Rinse them as the
  label directs and dispose of them as instructed — never burn them or leave
  them in the field or in a water source.
- **If poisoning is suspected (vomiting, blurred vision, sweating, breathing
  difficulty, twitching): stop work, move the person to fresh air, remove
  contaminated clothing, and take the person and the product label to a doctor
  or hospital immediately.** Do not wait for symptoms to worsen and do not
  induce vomiting unless a doctor says to.

## Spurious and substandard products

- Buy from a licensed dealer, keep the bill, and keep a small sample of the
  product and its label until harvest.
- Check the label for the registration number, batch number, and expiry date,
  and that the seal is intact.
- If a product does not work at the labelled dose, or you suspect it is
  spurious, report it to your agriculture officer rather than buying a second
  product to compensate.

## What FarmIQ can and cannot tell you

- FarmIQ can help you work out **what kind of problem you are facing**, what to
  check, how to choose the right class of product, how to rotate modes of
  action, and what safety steps are mandatory.
- FarmIQ does not recommend brands, active ingredients, or doses from memory.
  The authoritative dose is on the label, and the crop-wise recommendation is
  in your state's Package of Practices. Confirm with your KVK or block
  agriculture officer before spraying.
