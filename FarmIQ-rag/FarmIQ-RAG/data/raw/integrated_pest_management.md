# Integrated Pest and Disease Management (IPM)

FarmIQ Starter Reference — general principles of Integrated Pest Management.
This document deliberately does **not** list specific pesticide doses or
products. For any chemical decision, read the product label and confirm with
your local agricultural extension officer or KVK.

## What IPM is

Integrated Pest Management combines several control methods so that chemicals
are a last resort rather than the first response. The goal is to keep pest and
disease populations below the level that causes economic loss, while protecting
beneficial insects, human health, and the environment.

## The IPM steps

1. **Prevent.** Use healthy, certified seed; resistant or tolerant varieties;
   clean fields; balanced fertiliser; correct spacing and planting time; and
   crop rotation. Many problems are prevented before they start.
2. **Monitor.** Scout the field regularly (at least weekly) and identify the
   actual pest or disease. Do not treat on suspicion alone.
3. **Decide using thresholds.** Apply control only when pest numbers or disease
   severity cross the economic threshold — the point at which the value of the
   crop loss would exceed the cost of control.
4. **Act with the least-risk method first.** Prefer biological, cultural, and
   mechanical controls before chemicals.
5. **Evaluate.** Check whether the action worked and record it for next season.

## Monitoring and identification

- Walk a zig-zag pattern across the field and inspect plants at several points.
- Look at the underside of leaves, growing tips, and the base of the stem.
- Count pests or estimate the percentage of affected plants or leaf area.
- Use yellow sticky traps for sucking pests such as whitefly and aphids, and
  pheromone traps for pests like fruit borer and fall armyworm.
- Identify correctly: a beneficial insect killed by mistake can worsen a pest
  outbreak.

## Cultural and mechanical controls

- Rotate crops to break pest and disease life cycles.
- Remove and destroy infected plants and crop residues.
- Destroy alternate weed hosts around the field.
- Hand-pick and destroy egg masses and larvae where practical.
- Use traps, barriers, and netting for high-value crops.
- Adjust planting date to avoid peak pest periods where locally advised.

## Biological control

- Conserve natural enemies such as ladybird beetles, spiders, parasitic wasps,
  and predatory mites by avoiding unnecessary broad-spectrum insecticide use.
- Encourage flowering plants on bunds and borders that provide nectar and
  shelter for beneficial insects.
- Biological agents and biopesticides (for example, products based on Bacillus
  species or neem) can be used as part of a programme; follow label guidance
  and local recommendations.

## Pests and diseases of common crops (examples)

- **Rice:** stem borer, brown planthopper, leaf folder, bacterial leaf blight,
  blast.
- **Wheat:** aphids, termites, rusts, Karnal bunt.
- **Maize:** fall armyworm, stem borer.
- **Cotton:** pink bollworm, whitefly, jassids, sucking pests.
- **Soybean and pulses:** girdle beetle, pod borer, whitefly, yellow mosaic.
- **Groundnut:** leaf miner, white grub, tikka (leaf spot) disease.

For each, prevention and monitoring come first; disease problems are often
linked to moisture, spacing, and variety choice.

## Disease questions — important note

Symptoms in the field can resemble several different problems. FarmIQ explains
possible causes based on the available information; it does not diagnose
disease from text or a description alone. Confirm with field inspection or a
diagnostic laboratory before spending on treatments.

## Safe and responsible chemical use

- Treat chemicals as a last resort, after prevention, monitoring, and
  non-chemical methods.
- Always read and follow the product label: dose, timing, safety interval, and
  protective equipment.
- Rotate active ingredients/modes of action to slow resistance.
- Never mix products unless the label permits it.
- Keep children and animals away during and after spraying, and observe the
  pre-harvest interval.
- Never burn or dump empty pesticide containers in the field or water sources.
- If poisoning is suspected, seek medical help immediately and keep the
  container label for the doctor.
