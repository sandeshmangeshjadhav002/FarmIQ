# Major Crop Practices (India)

FarmIQ Starter Reference — general, seasonal practices for common Indian field
crops. These are broad guidelines; always follow your state's Package of
Practices (PoP) from the State Agricultural University and the crop-wise advice
on your Soil Health Card.

## General season names

- **Kharif** — monsoon season, sowing with the onset of the rains (roughly June
  to July), harvested in autumn.
- **Rabi** — winter season, sown around October to November, harvested in
  spring.
- **Zaid/Summer** — a short summer season between Rabi and Kharif for crops
  such as summer maize, vegetables, and fodder.

## Rice (paddy)

- Grown mainly in Kharif under assured water; requires standing water or
  frequent irrigation depending on the system of cultivation.
- Transplanting is common; direct-seeded rice (DSR) saves water and labour
  where supported.
- Key nutrients: nitrogen in splits, phosphorus at establishment, potassium
  and zinc as required (zinc deficiency is common under flooded conditions).
- Manage water carefully: alternate wetting and drying can save water without
  large yield penalty where advised.
- Watch for stem borer, brown planthopper, blast, and bacterial leaf blight.

## Wheat

- A Rabi crop sown in October–November; timely sowing is important for yield.
- Needs 4–6 irrigations at critical stages: crown root initiation (about 20–25
  days after sowing), tillering, jointing, flowering, milk, and dough stages.
- Balanced nitrogen in splits; avoid excess nitrogen, which increases lodging
  and disease.
- Watch for rusts, aphids, and termites.

## Maize

- Grown in Kharif, Rabi, and Zaid seasons depending on the region and water
  availability.
- Responsive to nitrogen; apply in splits and ensure adequate moisture at
  tasselling and silking (the most sensitive stages).
- Watch for fall armyworm (monitor early, especially young plants) and stem
  borer.

## Cotton

- A Kharif crop, commonly on medium to heavy soils.
- Sensitive to waterlogging and to early-season pest attack.
- Monitor closely for sucking pests early in the season and for pink bollworm
  (including using pheromone traps and timely termination of the crop).
- Do not extend the crop beyond its recommended period, which worsens
  pink bollworm.

## Soybean and pulses

- Mostly Kharif (soybean) or Rabi (chickpea, lentil) depending on the crop.
- As legumes they fix nitrogen but still benefit from balanced nutrition,
  including phosphorus and sulphur; pulses respond well to micronutrients such
  as zinc and boron.
- Avoid excess irrigation and waterlogging, which cause root diseases.
- Watch for pod borer, girdle beetle, and yellow mosaic virus (soybean).

## Groundnut

- Grown in Kharif and summer; prefers well-drained sandy loam soils.
- Calcium and gypsum at pegging (for pod development) are important, as are
  sulphur and zinc.
- Avoid heavy, compacted soils and waterlogged conditions.
- Watch for leaf miner, white grub, and tikka (leaf-spot) disease.

## General practices that benefit most crops

1. Use certified, treated seed of a recommended variety for your region.
2. Sow at the recommended time and seed rate.
3. Test soil and apply fertiliser according to the recommended dose.
4. Keep the field free of weeds early in the season; weeds compete strongly for
   water and nutrients.
5. Scout regularly for pests and diseases and act using IPM thresholds.
6. Irrigate at critical growth stages and avoid both stress and waterlogging.
7. Rotate crops and include legumes to protect soil health.
