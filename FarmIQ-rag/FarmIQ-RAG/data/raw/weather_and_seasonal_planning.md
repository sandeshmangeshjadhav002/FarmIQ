# Weather, Season, and Field Planning

FarmIQ Starter Reference — how weather and season affect the decisions you make
on the farm, and what to check before sowing, spraying, irrigating, or
harvesting. These are general guidelines; always follow your state's Package of
Practices (PoP) and your local Agromet Advisory Service.

## Why weather drives every field decision

Almost every operation on a farm is decided by two things: how much water is in
the soil, and what the air is about to do. Sowing into dry soil, spraying before
rain, or irrigating ahead of a downpour all waste money. The four weather
factors that matter most are:

1. **Rainfall** — total amount, how it is spread through the season, and heavy
   single-day spells.
2. **Temperature** — both day and night temperature, and sudden changes.
3. **Humidity** — high humidity favours most fungal and bacterial diseases.
4. **Wind** — decides whether you can spray, and how much water the crop loses.

## Getting a reliable forecast

- Use the **India Meteorological Department (IMD)** forecast for your district
  and the **Agromet Advisory Service** bulletins issued for your agro-climatic
  zone (these are prepared for farmers and give crop-stage-specific advice).
- **Kisan Call Centre — 1800-180-1551** (toll-free) for weather and crop advice
  in your language.
- **mKisan** SMS service and your state agriculture department's farmer portal.
- **FarmIQ cannot give you a live forecast.** It can only explain how to plan
  around weather — always confirm the actual numbers with the sources above
  before acting.

## Read the forecast before you act

| You are about to… | Check | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sow or transplant | Rain expected in the next 3–5 days; soil moisture | Seed needs moisture to germinate; sowing just before a good rain saves an irrigation |
| Apply fertiliser (especially nitrogen) | Heavy rain expected | Nitrogen is washed away by heavy rain; top-dress after rain, not before |
| Spray any crop protection product | Rain within 6–24 hours; wind speed; temperature | Rain washes the spray off; wind causes drift; high temperature causes scorching |
| Irrigate | Rain forecast in the next 24–48 hours | Irrigating before rain wastes water and can waterlog the field |
| Harvest | Rain forecast during harvest window | Wet grain/seed spoils in store; delay or arrange drying and cover |
| Dry the produce | 2–3 clear, sunny days | Produce must dry properly or it will mould in storage |

## Spraying and weather

- **Do not spray when rain is expected within a few hours** — the product is
  washed off before it works, and the run-off contaminates water.
- **Do not spray in strong wind** — the spray drifts onto neighbouring crops and
  the applicator.
- **Avoid the hottest part of the day** — many products cause leaf burn and
  volatilise, and the worker is at risk. Early morning or late afternoon is
  better.
- **High humidity and leaf wetness** mean disease pressure is rising — scout
  early rather than waiting for the first symptom.
- Apply the **right product at the right stage**; a spray aimed at the wrong
  growth stage will not control the pest even in perfect weather. For how to
  choose and apply a product safely, see the crop protection guidance in this
  knowledge base.

## Managing a dry spell (deficit rainfall)

- **Mulch** the soil surface with crop residue to reduce evaporation and keep
  the root zone cooler.
- **Prioritise irrigation to the critical stages** of each crop — for most
  cereals this is flowering and grain filling; stress at these stages costs the
  most yield.
- **Reduce plant population stress** by thinning where the crop is too thick;
  every plant then has more water.
- **Weed early and thoroughly** — weeds compete for the same limited water.
- Avoid heavy nitrogen doses during a drought; the crop cannot use them.
- For long dry spells, consider **short-duration or drought-tolerant varieties**
  of the same crop for the next season.

## Managing excess rain and waterlogging

- **Open drainage channels and field outlets** before the rainy season; most
  crop loss in heavy rain is caused by water standing for even 2–3 days.
- **Raised beds** for vegetables and for crops sensitive to waterlogging.
- Do not walk or drive on wet soil more than necessary — it compacts and
  destroys structure.
- After waterlogging, **check for root rot and nutrient leaching** (yellowing,
  stunted growth) and top-dress nitrogen once the soil drains.
- Some crops are far more sensitive to standing water than others — cotton,
  pulses, and most vegetables suffer quickly; rice tolerates it.

## Sowing window and season

- **Kharif** — sow with the onset of the monsoon; a delay of even 1–2 weeks
  shortens the season and reduces yield, so match the variety's duration to the
  time left.
- **Rabi** — sowing too late exposes the crop to rising temperature during grain
  filling; sow at the recommended time for your region.
- **Zaid/summer** — only where assured irrigation is available.
- Match the **variety duration** to the length of the season you actually have
  and to the expected date of the first rain or the first frost.

## Temperature and frost

- **Low temperature at flowering** causes flower drop, poor pollination, and
  sterile grain in many crops.
- **Frost** is a risk in winter in parts of central and northern India — light
  and frequent irrigation, smoke, and covering young nurseries reduce damage.
- **Very high temperature at flowering** causes similar losses; avoid sowing so
  that flowering falls in the hottest weeks.

## Planning calendar: what to think about, by stage

1. **Before sowing** — forecast for the sowing window, seed treatment, soil test
   and fertiliser plan, drainage ready.
2. **Establishment** — moisture for germination, weed control, gap filling.
3. **Vegetative growth** — first top-dressing after rain or irrigation, early
   pest scouting.
4. **Flowering** — protect from stress; this stage decides most of the yield;
   avoid spraying during peak flowering where pollinators are active.
5. **Grain/fruit filling** — adequate moisture, watch for pests attacking the
   produce itself.
6. **Maturity and harvest** — dry weather during harvest, arrange drying space
   and storage before the crop is ready.
7. **After harvest** — record what worked, plan the next season's calendar.

## Record keeping

Keep a simple notebook of sowing date, variety, fertiliser dates and amounts,
irrigation dates, spray dates, and weather events (heavy rain, dry spell,
frost). These records are what let you compare two seasons and see which
decisions paid off — and they help an extension officer advise you.
